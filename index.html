The Henderson–Whetro Family Story

Two families, one wedding

On November 15, 1977, two 18-year-olds took out a marriage license in Williams County, Ohio: the children's father, a Henderson, and their mother, a Whetro. Their marriage joined two families that had reached the same corner of northwest Ohio by very different roads.

Their mother's people had been farming around Alvordton since the 1890s. Her surname came from Germany by way of the Shenandoah Valley of Virginia, and her mother's side came from Ireland by way of Wisconsin and Ontario, Canada.

Their father's people were newer to the area. His surname came from Scotland to the farm country of Champaign County, Illinois, then to the city of Indianapolis. His mother's family lived in the little Paulding County village of Latty.

This booklet tells both stories, oldest first. It was put together in November 2026 from census records, death and marriage records, draft cards, Social Security files, newspaper obituary indexes and Find a Grave. Where something comes only from family trees or is not yet proven, the text says so.

1813–1930: The Whetros, from Germany to Alvordton

John Whetro of the Shenandoah Valley

John Whetro was born in Germany on March 1, 1813. Where in Germany, and when he crossed, are still unknown. By 1841 he was in Virginia, where his son Henry was born that December.

In the 1850 census John, 37, was farming in Shenandoah County, Virginia, with land worth $300. The census taker wrote the name as "Wetherall." At home were his wife Elizabeth, 35, and seven children: Elizabeth, Louisa, Henry, Margaret, Abigail, John and William. Family trees give Elizabeth's maiden name as Baum.

Sometime in the 1850s the family moved west to Madison Township in Richland County, Ohio, near Mansfield. By the 1860 census eleven children were at home, the youngest, Milton, just a year old. John died on January 1, 1868, and is buried in Mansfield Cemetery. Elizabeth lived until 1892.

Henry and Barbara

On November 9, 1866, in Richland County, Henry Whetro married Barbara Beilstein. Barbara was born on February 18, 1847; probably in Germany. The 1870 census says Württemberg, later censuses say Pennsylvania and Virginia, and family trees say Bavaria, which is where the 1870 census places her father. Clerks never agreed on her name, writing it Bistline, Bilestine and Balstein. The two families were close: Henry's sister Louisa also married a Beilstine.

Henry and Barbara moved northwest to Williams County. By 1900 Henry was farming in Mill Creek Township, near the village of Alvordton. Barbara died on July 7, 1902, and Henry on May 26, 1930, at Alvordton, at 88. Both are buried in Kunkle Cemetery. Their children included Charles Arthur, Mayme (later Crum) and Howard, who married in Ontario.

Charles, Maud and Carl

Charles Arthur Whetro was born in Williams County on October 2, 1874. On January 2, 1908, at 32, he married Maud Viola Schlosser. Maud was born on May 14, 1884, the daughter of Samuel Scott Schlosser and Ellen Whetstone, who had married in Williams County in 1883. Ellen had grown up in Springfield Township, the daughter of John and Hannah Whetstone. Their son Carl Henry was born at Alvordton on November 22, 1908, and in 1910 the family was farming in Mill Creek Township.

Carl lost both parents young. Maud died on September 24, 1916, at 32, when he was seven. Charles died on December 20, 1918, at 44, when Carl had just turned ten. Charles is buried in Walnut Grove Cemetery at Alvordton.

Carl and Virginia

On October 1, 1934, Carl married Virginia Catharine Gleason across the state line in Steuben County, Indiana. Virginia was born at Fayette, in Fulton County, on October 7, 1912. Her parents, Vernie Gleason and Ethel L. Fortney, had married in Fulton County in 1910, when he was 20 and she was 19. Vernie's father, George J. Gleason, was the son of George Gleason, a cooper from Massachusetts, and Julia Hand, who married in Steuben County, Indiana, in 1860. His mother, Cassie Smith, was born in Switzerland in 1863; she outlived her husband by thirty years and died at Fayette in 1940.

Carl's 1940 draft card describes him as 31, five foot five, 150 pounds, with black hair and blue eyes, working for the Gerity-Adrian company. In 1950 the family lived at Alvordton, where Carl worked 40 hours a week as a buffer in an auto parts factory. Their son Robert Owen was 15. They also had a daughter.

Carl died at Morenci, Michigan, on October 24, 1985, at 76. He is buried in Floral Grove Cemetery in Pioneer, Ohio, the same cemetery where Jamie's parents, Tony and Althea Bexten, are buried. Virginia lived to 94 and died on February 16, 2007.

1863–1950: The Tobins and Volways

From Ireland to Fond du Lac

Joanne Tobin's family began in America with two Irish immigrants, Thomas Tobin and Katharine "Kate" Kiley. Thomas was born about 1818; his Find a Grave memorial says at Waterford, though no record yet confirms it. Kate was born in 1839. By the late 1850s they were farming in Forest Township, Fond du Lac County, Wisconsin, where they raised at least six children. Thomas died in 1898 and Kate in 1920, and both are buried in Calvary Cemetery at Fond du Lac.

The hotel keeper of Fond du Lac

Their son James Tobin was born in Forest Township in June 1863.

In 1889 James married Mary Jane Hirton, who was born in New York. By 1900 James, 36, was a hotel keeper in Fond du Lac, Wisconsin. He owned the house at 22 Fourth Street, with a mortgage, and filled it with boarders. At home were his children Thomas, Lily, Edna and Alice, and a baby son, Joseph, born on June 14, 1899. James was still living in Fond du Lac in 1930.

Vera's parents

Vera Frances Volway was born on April 26, 1904, in Lambton County, Ontario, across the St. Clair River from Michigan. Her birth record indexes her parents as George Marcher Volway and Catherine Dasan Vansickle, but their own records show they were Joseph James Volway and Catherine Susan Vansickle, who married at Petrolia, Lambton County, on January 22, 1890.

The Valois of New France

Volway was the English spelling of Valois, a French-Canadian name. Joseph was born in 1860, at Cornwall, Ontario, according to his Catholic marriage record. In 1881 he was a labourer at Sarnia, living with his parents, John (Jean Baptiste) Valois and Christine Bériault. They had married in 1843 at the parish of St-Ignace in Coteau-du-Lac, Quebec, just downriver from Cornwall. Jean Baptiste died at Sarnia in 1885.

Quebec's church registers take the family back five more generations, each marriage naming the groom's parents. Jean Baptiste was the son of Rémi Valois and Josephte Lalonde, married at Vaudreuil in 1806. Rémi died young, in March 1822, and was buried at Pointe-Claire; Josephte was then pregnant with their last child. Rémi was the son of Jean Marie Valois and Marie Louise Legault, married at Pointe-Claire, on the island of Montreal, in 1786. Jean Marie's parents, Jean Baptiste Valois and Marie Josephe Dubois, married at Pointe-Claire in 1756, three years before the British took Quebec. His parents, Pierre Valois and Marie Clémence Girard, married at the Notre-Dame church in Montreal in 1724. And Pierre's parents, Jacques Valois and Marie Jeanne Couillard, married at Champlain, on the St. Lawrence, on February 22, 1694. This is the family's oldest documented line, and Quebec's Fichier Origine, which records where New France's settlers came from, takes it one step further, across the Atlantic.

From Saintonge to New France

Jacques was born Jacques Levallois on December 21, 1672, at Saint-Hérie, in the parish of Matha, in the old province of Saintonge in western France, between La Rochelle and Cognac. He was baptized on January 15, 1673, at the Protestant temple of Thors, near Matha; his family were Huguenots. His father, Hélie Levallois, was a merchant and the clerk of the county of Matha, and his mother was Judith Merlet.

In the 1680s King Louis XIV was forcing France's Protestants to convert, and in 1685 he outlawed their faith. About 1684, still a boy, Jacques crossed to New France, where he served as a soldier in the Troupes de la Marine, the colony's garrison. In New France he became Catholic, as every settler had to, and took the name Valois, with the soldier's nickname Grandchamp. After Marie Jeanne Couillard died he married Marie-Marguerite Carpentier in 1706, and he died on July 19, 1750, at Île-Dupas on the St. Lawrence, at 77.

The wives' families

The women who married into the Valois family had French roots of their own. Pierre Valois's wife, Marie Clémence Girard, was baptized at Lachine in 1694 and died at Pointe-Claire in 1772, at 79. She was the daughter of Léon Girard, a miller's son from Aujac, only a few miles from Matha in Saintonge. He had come to New France about 1684, the same year as Jacques, and married Clémence Beaune at Lachine in 1688. Clémence, baptized at Montreal in 1671, was the daughter of Jean Beaune dit Lafranchise, from Bellenaves in the Bourbonnais, who had married Marie-Madeleine Bougery at Quebec City in 1667.

Jean Marie Valois's wife, Marie Louise Legault dit Deslauriers, came from a Breton line. Her great-grandfather Noël Le Goff was born about 1674 at Irvillac, in Brittany, the son of a farmer. He came to New France as a soldier about 1695, married Marie Bénard at Montreal in 1698, and became Noël Legault dit Deslauriers. His son and grandson, both named Charles, farmed at Pointe-Claire, where the Legaults and Valois lived side by side. The younger Charles married Marie Louise Pilon in 1759, and through her the family reaches Normandy: her great-grandfather Antoine Pilon, a butcher's son from Bayeux, came to New France about 1688 and married Marie-Anne Brunet at Montreal in 1689.

The church records also name the fathers of two more wives: Josephte Lalonde's father, Jean Baptiste Lalonde of Vaudreuil, and Christine Bériault's parents, Joseph Bériault and a mother written as Marie Saune. Marie Jeanne Couillard was the daughter of Pierre Couillard, born in Angoumois, France, in 1639, and Jeanne Bilodeau, who married at Trois-Rivières in 1666.

The Vansickles and Bells

Catherine Susan Vansickle was born on June 23, 1869, in Saginaw County, Michigan. Her father, Eugene Vansickle, was born in Canada about 1847, and her mother, Victoria Bell, in St. Clair County, Michigan, about 1845. In 1850 Victoria was a girl at Port Huron in the large family of William and Catharine J. Bell. Eugene and Victoria married at Port Huron in 1868, lived at Saginaw in 1870, and by 1881 had moved across the river to Petrolia, Ontario, then a booming oil town. Catherine died in 1955 and is buried beside Joseph at Mount Calvary Cemetery in Wyoming, Ontario.

Joseph and Vera

Joseph Tobin and Vera Volway married in Michigan. A Detroit border-crossing record lists the two of them together, and their daughter Joanne was born in Ontario in 1938, according to family trees. They raised at least six daughters, Joanne among them.

By 1950 the Tobins were living at Alvordton, Ohio, the Whetros' village. Vera died on November 8, 1971, and Joseph at Defiance in September 1974, at 75. They are buried in Pleasant View Union Cemetery at Fayette.

Robert and Joanne

Robert Owen Whetro, Carl and Virginia's son, was born at Fayette on March 5, 1935. He and Joanne Tobin grew up in the same small village: both families were at Alvordton in 1950. They married on October 16, 1954, when Robert was 19.

Robert and Joanne raised a large family at Alvordton: thirteen children, nine daughters and four sons, all living, so they are not named here.

Joanne died at Fort Wayne, Indiana, on July 24, 2001. Robert died at Alvordton on November 25, 2003, at 68, and is buried in Pleasant View Union Cemetery at Fayette, near Joanne's parents.

1887–1950: The Hendersons, from Scotland to Indianapolis

A Scot in Champaign County

The Henderson name in the family comes from Scotland. John Henderson, born there about 1846, settled in Champaign County, Illinois. In 1876, at 30, he married Mary Ann Wolpert. Their sons were David Alexander, born in 1877, and John V. Henderson, born on November 1, 1887. Family trees give John V.'s middle name as Valentine, after his grandfather.

Mary Ann was born in Pennsylvania about 1857, the daughter of Valentine Wolpert and Mary Ann Smith. Valentine was born about 1820 in Baden, in southwest Germany, and in 1870 he farmed in Tazewell County, Illinois, with eight children at home. A baptism at Gommersdorf, Baden, on January 10, 1820, is probably his.

The elder John died young. In the 1900 census Mary was a widow, heading the household in Hensley Township with David, 22, and John, 12, still in school. She later married Nicholas Leischner and died at Urbana in 1921.

John V. and Gertrude

In 1908, at 21, John V. married Gertrude B. Lemmon in Champaign County. Gertrude was born in Indiana on March 20, 1889, the daughter of John A. Lemmon and Emma Jane Beeler, who had married in Pike County, Indiana, in 1879. They lived at Urbana in 1920 and raised four children: Mildred, John Nicholas, Helen and Katherine. John Nicholas was born at Champaign on January 16, 1914.

By 1930 the family had moved to Indianapolis, to a house on Illinois Street. In 1940 they rented on West Twentieth Street, where John V., 53, worked as a pressman and earned $2,400 that year. He died in Tucson, Arizona, on April 17, 1963, and was buried in Highland Lawn Cemetery at Terre Haute, Indiana. Gertrude died in Santa Clara County, California, in 1974.

John Nicholas and Eleanor

On September 9, 1933, in Indianapolis, John Nicholas Henderson, 19, married Eleanor Augusta Frantzreb, 18. The marriage record calls him John Nicholson Henderson; every other record says Nicholas.

Eleanor was born in Indianapolis on January 8, 1915. Her father, William Christian Frantzreb, was born on October 4, 1865, at Five Points in Allen County, Indiana, near Fort Wayne, and is buried in Indianapolis's Concordia Cemetery. Her mother was probably Emma Kuhlman, born in Indiana in 1876, the daughter of Henry Frederick Kuhlman, an Indianapolis carpenter and later flour dealer, and Eleanor Gabert.

John and Eleanor's son James Edward Henderson was born in Indianapolis on June 13, 1934. The marriage did not last. By 1940 John had a new wife, Dorothy Helen, and was working at Allison Engineering in Indianapolis. He enlisted in the Army at Fort Benjamin Harrison on April 22, 1942. Eleanor later married Richard Henry Sellers, according to family trees. She died in April 1986.

1892–2013: The Dunstans of Latty, and James and Mary

Ralph and Opal

John Ralph Dunstan was born at Van Wert, Ohio, on March 1, 1892, the son of Stephen Dunstan and Marguerite Dally. Opal Libby Powell was born on August 10, 1904, at O'Fallon in St. Charles County, Missouri, the daughter of Charles E. Powell and Martha Jane Radabaugh. Opal had been married before, to a Mr. Vogel, and had several Vogel children. Ralph's mother, Margaret, was the daughter of John Lane Dally, a carpenter turned farmer, and Mary Catherine George, who married in Hardin County, Ohio, in 1868. In 1900 Margaret, a dressmaker, and eight-year-old Ralph were living with her parents near Middle Point, in Van Wert County; Stephen was not with them.

Their daughter Mary Catherine Dunstan was born in Dayton on March 27, 1935, and a son, John R., followed about 1937. Ralph and Opal married in Indiana on October 15, 1936.

The family settled in Latty, a small village in Paulding County. In 1940 Ralph, 48, had a year of college and worked as an engineering clerk in the local WPA office; he owned their house, valued at $500. In 1950 he was a laborer for the state highway department. Ralph died at Fort Wayne on June 13, 1963, at 71. Opal, who also went by the names Leslie and Ludwig after later marriages, died at Paulding on August 21, 1990.

James and Mary

James Edward Henderson's teenage years were unsettled. In 1951 his Social Security record lists him under his stepfather's surname, as James Edward Sellers. He enlisted in the Army on August 1, 1950, at only 16, and was discharged that September 18.

James came to northwest Ohio and married Mary Catherine Dunstan of Latty. They had three children, one of them the Henderson children's father. The marriage ended in divorce. James died in Glendale, Arizona, on November 21, 1992, at 58; his obituary ran in the Defiance Crescent-News.

Mary later married Cornelius "Curt" Classen, who had been married before and was born in Meade County, Kansas, in 1918. They married in Fulton County on May 15, 1983, and lived in Archbold, where Curt died in 1995. Mary died at Archbold on March 22, 2013, at 77, and is buried in Archbold Cemetery.

The parents

The children's parents were both 18 when they married in Williams County on November 15, 1977. Their father's father had come from Indianapolis and his mother from Latty; their mother had grown up in Alvordton among Whetro and Tobin relatives. They had six children, all living, so they are not named in this public copy.

So the children's ancestry runs back to Scotland through the Hendersons, Ireland through the Tobins and Kileys, and Germany through the Whetros, the Wolperts of Baden, the Frantzrebs and Barbara Beilstein, and Switzerland through Cassie Smith Gleason. Their oldest documented line is French: Jacques Levallois of Matha in Saintonge, who came to New France about 1684. His descendants, the Valois, became the Volways of Ontario.

Still to discover

Some questions are still open. Letters, a family Bible, old photographs or a story the children heard growing up may hold the answers.

  • Where in Scotland was John Henderson born, and when did he come? One online tree says Gamrie, Banffshire, in 1846, which fits his age, but it mixes him up with another man. Scottish parish records would settle it.
  • Which Lalonde and Bériault families did the Valois wives come from? Their own baptisms would settle it.
  • Who were Hélie Levallois's and Judith Merlet's parents? The registers of Matha and Thors, online at the Charente-Maritime archives, may say.
  • Were the Tobins from Waterford? Thomas Tobin's memorial says so, without a source. Catholic records in Fond du Lac may confirm it.
  • Where in Germany was John Whetro from? Church records in Shenandoah County, Virginia, or Richland County, Ohio, sometimes name a home village.
  • When and where did Joseph Tobin and Vera Volway marry, and how long did they live in Ontario before moving to Alvordton?
  • Who were the parents of Mary Jane Hirton, Stephen Dunstan and Charles Powell?
  • Where in Germany was Barbara Beilstein born? The 1870 census says Württemberg; family trees say Bavaria.

    Henderson–Whetro Family History Report

    Summary

    The ancestors of the six Henderson children are traced on every branch to their great-great-grandparents, and on several lines further, to immigrants from Scotland, Ireland and Germany. Their longest line, the French-Canadian Valois (Volway), is documented in Quebec church registers back to a 1694 marriage at Champlain, and Fichier Origine traces its founder, Jacques Levallois, to Matha in Saintonge, France, where he was born in 1672. Their father descends from a Scottish-born John Henderson of Champaign County, Illinois. Their mother descends from John Whetro, born in Germany in 1813, who farmed in Shenandoah County, Virginia, before moving to Richland County, Ohio.

    How to use the app

    The Henderson–Whetro Family Explorer is the family history of the six Henderson children, traced back as far as ten generations. It is built on the same app as the Bexten Family Explorer. Compiled in November 2026 and updated as new records come in. Tap a topic to open it.

    Getting started
    • Keep it on your phone. On iPhone, open the link in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On Android, open it in Chrome, tap ⋮, then Add to Home screen or Install app. It then opens full-screen like any app.
    • Works offline. Once opened, the app keeps a copy on your phone, so the tree, story and charts work without a connection. Maps, record links and directions need the internet.
    • Updates. New research shows up the next time you open the app while online. If you don't see a change, close the app completely and open it again.
    • Living people. The children and their parents are named but their dates are left out. This copy is private to you.
    Getting around
    • Bottom menu. Six tabs: Tree, Map, Story, Report, Charts and To-do.
    • Top bar. On the home screen you see the full title. Everywhere else a slim bar shows where you are, like “Charts · Lifetimes” or the person you're looking at, and stays at the top as you scroll. The ? button opens this help.
    • Back to where you were. When you open a person from a chart, the map, the index or anywhere else, their card starts with a “‹ Back to …” button and a trail of the people you've visited. Tap Back to return to the same chart, the same spot on the page and the same filters. Tap any name in the trail to jump back to that person. The iPhone swipe-back gesture works too.
    • Tap names. Almost every name in the app, on charts, lists, maps and timelines, opens that person's card.
    Tree tab
    • Home screen. Starts with the children, their parents and grandparents, and a one-line This week reminder when a family birth, wedding or death falls in the next seven days; tap it to see them all. On the day itself it shows as On this day. When nothing is coming up, it doesn't appear.
    • Find a person. Type any part of a name in the search box and pick from the list.
    • Family index (the list button). Every surname A–Z with what the name means and who carried it, and every place, grouped by state or country, with who was born, lived, married, died or was buried there.
    • Share (the arrow button). A QR code to scan, plus buttons to send or copy the link with instructions for adding the app to a home screen.
    • Home (the house button). Back to the children, and clears the back trail.
    A person's card
    • Name, dates and side. A gold dot means Dad's side, lavender means Mom's side. “From records” or “Unproven” says how solid the person is.
    • Relationship. How the person is related to the children, like “2nd great-grandfather,” and how many generations back. How they're connected shows the line from the children up to this person.
    • Facts. Birth, marriage, work, military service, death and burial, as found in records.
    • Parent of links down to the child in our line.
    • Buried at the cemetery; tap it to see the cemetery on the graves map, where you'll also find Directions.
    • Brothers and sisters, when known.
    • The name. What the surname means and where it comes from. Names that can't be pinned down are marked “Not certain.”
    • Record checklist. Birth, parents, marriage, census, immigration, death and burial, each marked ✓ (record linked), ◐ (known, but its own record isn't linked yet) or ○ (not found yet), with a “Where to look” tip for anything missing.
    • See the records. Tiles that open the actual records online: census pages, certificates, Find a Grave memorials, FamilySearch and more.
    • Sources. Every record and reference behind the card.
    • Ancestors. Parents, grandparents and great-grandparents. Tap anyone to move up the tree.
    Map tab
    • Journeys. Two maps, Europe (where the families left from) and America (where they settled), with the routes each family line took. Tap a line's button to show only that family. Arrows off the edge of a map are Atlantic crossings; dashed routes are uncertain.
    • Graves. A map of the eighteen cemeteries that hold family: nine in northwest Ohio, one at Mansfield, four in Indiana, one in Illinois, one in Wisconsin and two in Ontario. Use Northwest Ohio, Farther away or All to zoom, and the Day trip button for a driving route between the northwest Ohio cemeteries. Each cemetery lists who is buried there, with Directions. The first time you tap Directions you choose Apple Maps or Google Maps; change it any time with “Directions open in” at the top of the Graves page.
    Story tab
    • Story. The family history told as a book, from Germany, Scotland and Ireland to Williams County. Use Jump to a chapter to skip ahead.
    • Timeline. Every dated event in order. Filter by Everything, Dad's side, Mom's side, or Family only (leaving out world events).
    Report tab
    • The full research report: what was found, where, how sure it is, what conflicts between records, and what's still open. Use Jump to a section to move around.
    Charts tab

    Nine charts, chosen with the buttons at the top.

    • Fan chart. Five generations in a half-circle, Dad's side left and Mom's side right. Tap anyone to center the fan on them and see their ancestors; tap the middle to open that person. Back and Start over step out again. Use + and − to zoom.
    • Bowtie. Four generations each way in a sideways tree. Use ‹ Dad's side, Center and Mom's side › to move across, or swipe. Turning your phone sideways helps.
    • Lines. Each long family line traced from its earliest ancestor down to the children.
    • Origins. How much of the children's ancestry comes from each part of the world, worked out from the tree, and when each line arrived in America.
    • Lifetimes. A bar for each ancestor's life against the Civil War, both World Wars and the Depression. Filter by side.
    • Families. How many children each couple in our line had, as far as records show.
    • Complete. How many ancestors have been found in each generation, out of how many there are.
    • Numbers. Family statistics: how many ancestors, generations, the longest life, birthplaces and common first names.
    • Cousins. The brothers and sisters of each ancestor. Their descendants are the family's cousins: the further back the ancestor, the more distant the cousin. Living cousins aren't named.
    To-do tab
    • The research still to do: record requests to send, visits to make and optional extras. For each record request, tap Not sent, Sent or Answered; the app keeps the dates and lets you add a note about the fee or what came back. Tick the other items off as you go. Everything is saved on this phone.
    • The request letters are in the Henderson–Whetro Research To-Do doc. When a record or reply comes back, send it to Claude to read and add.
    Colors and symbols

    • Lavender Mom's side (Whetro, Gleason, Tobin, Volway and others).
    • Amber outline or “Unproven” a likely connection that no record has confirmed yet.
    • Dashed box an ancestor not found yet.
    • ✓ ◐ ○ on the record checklist: record linked, known but not linked, not found yet.
    What's new and the family book

    • Family book.
